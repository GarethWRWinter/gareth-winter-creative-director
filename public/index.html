Why me

There's a tired old idea that work can be beautiful or it can sell, but not both. I've spent twenty years cheerfully proving that's nonsense.

I'm a Creative Director and Head of Content who can also read a P&L without breaking into a sweat. Right now I run the entire creative output of one of the world's best-known personal-growth platforms, working straight with the CEO and steering more than a million dollars of ad spend a month, so "reads a P&L" isn't a figure of speech. I write, shoot, direct and design, and I build the AI systems that let a small team punch like a big one. Mostly, though, I hire people who are better than me and try to stay out of their way. Every brand on this page ended up with work worth sharing and numbers worth showing. That, in the end, is the whole job.

What I deliver

Four jobs, one person, and the rather useful fact that they all talk to each other.

01

Creative Direction

Set the standard, then hold the line.

I own the vision and the craft to back it up: identity, art direction, film, design, the lot. I've never asked a team to clear a bar I couldn't clear myself, which keeps everyone honest, me included. Twenty years in and I'm still behind the camera and in the edit at midnight. Standards aren't a slide you present once and forget about. They're a habit you keep when nobody's watching.

02

Content & Marketing

A Head of Content who can read a P&L.

Lovely work that doesn't move a number is a hobby, and I've got enough hobbies. I build content that's wired to the commercials: positioning, channel strategy, the full funnel. Then I show you the exact point on the graph where it started paying for itself. Treating craft and growth as enemies has always struck me as a clever way to fail at both.

03

AI Transformation

Build the machine, keep the soul.

I design AI-powered creative systems, and the rather unglamorous plumbing underneath them, so teams can ship more without shipping worse. Used well, AI clears away the busywork and protects the bit that matters. Used badly, it carpet-bombs the internet with beige. I have strong opinions about which of those we should be doing.

04

Leadership & Culture

People do brave work when they feel safe.

I build teams from scratch and cultures people actually want to stay in: clarity instead of politics, honesty delivered kindly, and enough rope for good people to surprise you (themselves included). I care about the work because I care about the people making it. I've never managed to separate the two, and I've long since stopped trying.

Artificial intelligence

Human-first AI, built by someone
who still does the work.

Most AI conversations are either breathless or terrified. Mine is neither, mostly because I'm too busy actually using the stuff. I treat it as creative infrastructure: a way to take the donkey-work out of production so people stay free for the part that matters, which is taste, judgement, and knowing when something is good enough to ship.

Systems, not gimmicks

I build repeatable creative pipelines, from research to generation to review to shipping, so a small team can produce like a big one without the quality quietly falling off a cliff.

Human-first by design

AI handles the volume and the endless variations. People keep the taste, the judgement and the final word. Nobody hands the brand's keys to an algorithm and wanders off for a coffee.

Proven in a real business

This isn't a LinkedIn thought-leadership thread. I'm doing it now, in production, for a platform serving three million people. An AI-led launch I shaped drove a 77% jump in new members, so the systems earn their keep.

I've never once worked on a brand I didn't believe in. Not by luck, and not for want of offers. It's simply the one rule I've never been willing to break.

About

Sport built the discipline. Creativity built the brands.

In 2012, Sir Bradley Wiggins became the first Briton to win the Tour de France, then went and won Olympic gold a few weeks later in London for good measure. I was at Sky, part of the team that told that story to the nation. Within ten years, 1.7 million people in the UK had got on a bike for the first time. That is what storytelling can do when it earns its place, and I've been chasing the feeling ever since.

Team Sky ran on one idea: marginal gains. Improve every element by one percent and the compound effect is faintly ridiculous. James Clear later built half of Atomic Habits around it. For me it was never a concept in a book. It was just Tuesday. It has been the operating system behind everything I've built since.

I'm not the corner-office sort of director. I shoot, write, direct, edit, design and produce, and I lead teams who are frankly better than me at all of it, which is exactly the point. At Mindvalley I make work for three million people across a hundred and ninety countries who are genuinely trying to change their lives. They can smell a phoney a mile off, and they'll share anything that's real. I believe in earning trust before asking for the sale, and in doing fewer things properly rather than lots of things badly. So I keep it honest, partly on principle and partly because it is simply how I'd rather work.
